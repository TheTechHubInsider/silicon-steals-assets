Single-board computers have long been dominated by ARM chips like the Raspberry Pi, but homelab enthusiasts frequently hit walls with software compatibility, thermal throttling, and lack of PCIe expansion. The ZimaBoard 2 1664 shatters those limitations by marrying Intel's efficient Alder Lake-N architecture with an open PCIe slot in a fanless, credit-card sized profile.

At the heart of the board is the Intel N150 quad-core processor (boosting up to 3.6 GHz) paired with a massive 16GB of soldered LPDDR5 memory. Unlike low-spec SBCs that choke on heavy containerized workloads, the ZimaBoard 2 effortlessly hosts Home Assistant OS, Pi-hole, Plex Media Server, Tailscale, and automated local databases without breaking a sweat.

The crowning engineering achievement of the ZimaBoard line is its exposed PCIe 3.0 x4 slot. Rather than locking users into fixed onboard chips, this standard desktop slot allows you to insert quad-port 10GbE NICs, NVMe carrier cards, SAS RAID controllers, or low-profile GPUs directly onto the board. Combined with two native SATA 6Gb/s ports and dual 2.5GbE Ethernet jacks, it is a micro-server powerhouse.

Acoustic and thermal engineering is sublime. The entire upper surface is sculpted from black anodized aluminum heatsink fins that dissipate heat purely through natural convection. The device operates in 100% absolute silence with zero fan noise, consuming between 6W at idle and 15W under full compute load.

Out of the box, the unit boots into CasaOS—a clean, app-store-styled personal cloud dashboard that turns Docker container deployment into a 1-click experience. However, because it is standard x86-64 hardware, you can easily install Proxmox VE, TrueNAS SCALE, pfSense, or standard Debian Linux via USB.

At $419.90 for the maxed-out 16GB RAM model, the ZimaBoard 2 is the most capable, versatile, and aesthetically refined fanless micro-server available for homelabbers and self-hosting purists.
